About

An edit and an interface are the same problem: someone is giving you their attention, and you owe them something worth it.

I studied cinema editing at Soore University in Tehran and spent six years cutting short films, ads, and branded content — 25+ projects, 15+ clients. For three of those years I also taught filmmaking to high-school students, which is where I learned that if you can't explain why a shot is in the film, it isn't.

I'm now in the Netherlands doing a master's in Digital Media Studies at Tilburg University, and I've moved that same instinct into product design. On Extendsell I work on add-ons and frequently bought together for Shopify merchants — a product with two users who want opposite things, which is exactly the kind of problem I like.

The motion design background isn't a detour. Knowing how something moves — how a transition carries meaning, how timing changes what a user believes — makes interfaces better in a way static mockups never show.

How I work

An editor's process, applied to product.

01

Watch first

Before changing anything, I sit with the raw material — users, tickets, the existing flow — and find where attention breaks.

02

Find the through-line

Every film has one story it's actually telling. So does every product. Name it, and the scope decisions make themselves.

03

Cut

Prototype, test, and remove. If a screen doesn't move the story forward, it leaves — no matter how much work went into it.

04

Watch it with an audience

An edit isn't finished until someone else has seen it. Ship, observe real use, and cut again.
