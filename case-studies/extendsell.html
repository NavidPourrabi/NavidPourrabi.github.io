Extendsell

A Shopify app that helps stores sell a bit more per order. I work on what we build and who it's for, and I design what store owners and shoppers see.

My role
Product and UX
Team
Small, remote
Platform
Shopify
Tools
Figma, Figma Motion

Two people, two different wishes

Every screen in this app has two users. The store owner wants the offer to be seen. The shopper just wants to buy their dress and doesn't want to feel tricked into anything. If a widget only works for one of them, it doesn't really work, because a shopper who feels tricked doesn't come back.

So the rule I kept coming back to was simple: the offer has to be clear about what it costs, what you save, and where things end up. If I couldn't explain a screen to both of them in one sentence, I changed the screen.

What shoppers see

Four widgets live on the product page, plus a quick view and an upsell after checkout. They take their colours and fonts from the store's own theme, so they look like part of the store instead of an app stuck on top. I designed them in light and dark, at desktop, tablet and phone sizes.

Add-ons

Small extras you can add one by one, like a case with a phone. Each card has its own price, size and button, and the line under the title says where the add-ons go, so nothing is a surprise at checkout.

Frequently bought together

A bundle you can edit. If you don't want the belt, untick it and the total updates. Everything else in the offer stays. Every item keeps its own size picker, because a bundle is useless if you can't choose your own size.

Related products and quick view

This one isn't selling a bundle. It helps a shopper find something that suits them better. It shows "page 1 of 4" so you know how much there is, and clicking a product opens a quick view, so you never lose your place on the page.

Volume discounts

The newest one: buy more of the same thing, pay less per item. I built it on the same pieces as frequently bought together, so it feels like the same app.

Prices are totals
Each tier shows what you'll actually pay for that many, with the old price crossed out under it. Nobody has to do maths.
One saving tag per tier
A single yellow tag says how much you save. One label, one job.
The store's own colour
The selected tier uses the theme's buy-button colour, so it matches the add to cart button under it.

What store owners see

Most of our users aren't designers, and they have a shop to run. So the settings had to be boring in a good way. Set things once, change them for one widget only if you need to, and always know what the other options are without opening every menu.

A safe default
Position is "Automatic (recommended)". Most stores never need to change it.
Set once, override when needed
A widget can say "use common setting", so a store changes something in one place instead of five.
Honest about limits
Colours and fonts follow the theme. If a store wants more, there's a contact us button instead of fifty sliders.

How it got there

Before any colour I did wireframes for all three screen sizes, mostly to figure out how the carousels should behave. Then I made three visual directions and showed them to the team as a real choice: one led by our brand, one that blends into the store's theme, one that pushes the offer.

The product side

Nice widgets don't matter if nobody installs the app. We have time and almost no budget, so a lot of my work is deciding where that time goes.

Who to talk to first

I made a list of 110 Shopify stores and matched each one with the feature that fits it best, and the language its store is in. That decided who we contacted first and what we led with. I wrote the outreach messages from there.

Small goals on purpose

Our first goal was 10 conversations with store owners and 3 stores actually using the app. Small enough to reach next to a master's, big enough to learn what to build next.

Getting through App Store review

Shopify reviewed the app and asked for changes. Those went to the top of the list, before any new feature. No listing, no installs.

Launching without a budget

For the Product Hunt launch I did my part of the outreach every day. For Black Friday we're giving six months free to stores that install before it, with a set of campaign images to go with it.

A name and a look

We renamed the app from ExtenSell to Extendsell, so I worked on the logo and a set of brand colours in Figma. Then I designed the App Store listing images, and I'm animating the listing video in Figma Motion.

Seeing what works

Store owners get one dashboard with views, clicks and sales for each offer, so they can see which ones are worth keeping.
